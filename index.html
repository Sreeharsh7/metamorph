Fold-switching proteins

Most proteins fold into one structure set by their amino-acid sequence. Fold-switching proteins break that rule: the same chain remodels its secondary and tertiary structure in response to a cellular cue, such as a binding partner, temperature, salt or the time of day, and flips between two stable, functional folds. They control transcription, circadian clocks, immune signalling and cell division. They are also a hard case for structure predictors that expect one sequence to give one structure, which makes them a frontier for computational biology.

Every character here is drawn from the Cα coordinates of two experimentally determined structures. The morph between them is a coarse-grained, constraint-relaxed path computed in your browser: virtual bonds keep their length, the chain never passes through itself, and segments that change partners swing around the core. It is an illustration, not a simulation of the real pathway.
